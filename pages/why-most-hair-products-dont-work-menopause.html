Menopause changes your hair at the follicle, but most products never get that far. Here's what does.

You've handled the hot flashes, the 3 a.m. wake-ups and the mood swings. Now it's the meno-hair, and whenever you bring it up, you hear the same thing: it's just part of getting older.

While it's true that menopause changes your hair, that doesn't mean you just have to accept it. Maybe you started noticing hair in the drain this month and don't know where to start; or maybe you've already tried everything and spent more than you'd like to admit. The reason real solutions are hard to come by is that most of what's being sold isn't built for what menopause actually does to your hair.

Hair Science

As estrogen levels drop, two things happen under your scalp:

  1. hair follicles spend less time in the growth phase, and over time, more shift into the resting phase before eventually shedding
  2. the follicles themselves shrink, so each new hair grows in finer and shorter than the last

Hair products that don't target this follicle system are not properly formulated to support your hair journey.

Hair Science: Why Topical Minoxidil Might Not Work for You

Topical minoxidil needs to be "turned on" by an enzyme produced in your hair follicles and some people simply don't produce enough. If you've tried topical minoxidil and it didn't work for you, that could be why.

This is the one your doctor will mention first, and for good reason: it's currently the only FDA-approved treatment for female pattern hair loss. But when you ask women who've actually used it, the same complaints come up:

It comes with several side effects. The most notable ones include the dread shed (the initial period of shedding that occurs when you start using minoxidil), scalp irritation, and toxicity to pets.

It's high maintenance. You apply it twice a day and it requires a few hours to fully absorb into your skin; it can leave your hair greasy, flat or crunchy, make styling a mess, and rub off on your pillow at night. Plenty of women quit because it doesn't fit their routine, not because it wasn't working.

The effects disappear once you stop. The warning is on the label, but many people don't realize that once you stop using minoxidil, the shedding comes right back, as if you were never using the drug in the first place.

TBB TLDRFDA-approved, but comes with a list of side effects, won't work for some people, and the results you do see only last as long as you keep applying it.

Hers is the most popular and top rated oral minoxidil option right now and if you've browsed channels like r/femalehairloss and r/menopause, you've seen that oral minoxidil is the one many women swear by. In clinical tests, it worked just as well as topical minoxidil, but for many, the oral pill is an easier fit for their routine. That being said, oral minoxidil comes with its own unique side effects:

It's a prescription drug. Oral minoxidil originated as a blood pressure medication, and hair growth happened to be a side effect. Doctors usually check your heart history before prescribing because it can increase your heart rate.

It can cause unwanted hair growth. When taken orally, minoxidil travels through your whole body, and can wake up hair everywhere (upper lip, chin, legs, etc.) — not just the hair on your head.

The effects disappear once you stop. Just like the topical version, once you stop taking minoxidil pills, the shedding comes right back, as if you were never using it in the first place.
