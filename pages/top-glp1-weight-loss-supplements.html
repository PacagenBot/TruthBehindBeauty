The Top 8 “GLP-1” Weight Loss Supplements. Which is the Real Deal?

From a $25 pharmacy pack to a celebrity capsule, here are 8 GLP-1 support products worth knowing about, and the one that both worked and fit into my day.

By Claire Bennett
Share:
Published Sep 22, 2026  ·  7 min read

If you’ve looked into GLP-1 this year, you know the options feel endless. There are shots, capsules, gummies, powders and packets, and almost every one has “GLP-1” printed somewhere on the label.

Here’s the short version.

GLP-1 is a hormone your gut releases after you eat. It tells your brain you’ve had enough and helps keep blood sugar steady after a meal. Prescription medications copy that hormone at a strength no supplement can match, and any brand that suggests otherwise is overselling.

What a good GLP-1 support supplement can do is facilitate the fullness signals your body already makes. That depends on which ingredients are in it and how much of each. It also depends on whether you’ll actually take it every day. In my experience, most people don’t just quit because a product failed. They also quit because they didn’t want to take it after day five. I tried or dug into 8 of the most talked-about options. Here’s where each one landed.

Best Overall Editor’s Pick 🏆

Wildtype GLP-1 Support Tea

Buy Now
Pros
  • Developed and founded by Harvard/Princeton scientists
  • Clinically studied ingredients at research-backed doses
  • Drinkable format, not a pill
  • Natural ingredients
  • Multiple functions (prebiotic, actives)
  • Really good flavor (lemon ginger)
  • Upwards of 700 clinician recommendations
  • No added sugar or artificial ingredients
Cons
  • Newer brand
  • Only available online
  • Can is not super portable
  • Mixing required (20s)
  • Only one flavor

I’ll be upfront: I didn’t expect a tea to top this list. I heard about Wildtype from a friend and assumed it would be a nice drink with a light sprinkle of active ingredients. The label and experience changed my mind.

Did some digging: The formula is simple and easy to read. It’s built around six studied ingredients: lemon verbena and hibiscus extracts that are standardized in specific botanical compounds that have been studied for appetite and weight management, prebiotic fiber that feeds your gut bacteria and adds a little fullness, rounded out with ginger for digestion and L-carnitine, an amino acid your body uses to turn fat into energy.

The part I didn’t expect to care about was the routine. It all sits on a jasmine green tea base. I make it around 2pm, which used to be my snack hour. Having something to sip at that time has done more for my consistency than any capsule I’ve tried. Add that to the active ingredient list and I was sold.

Best Capsule

Lemme Reset

(formerly GLP-1 Daily)

Buy Now
Pros
  • Three branded ingredients (doses listed)
  • Stimulant-free
  • Easy to find in stores and online
Cons
  • Premium price, around $90 for a one-time bottle
  • Capsule format harder to maintain consistency
  • Two capsules a day
  • Brand recommends 3 to 6 months before judging results

Lemme is the celebrity-backed option (via Kourtney Kardashian), and at first glance to its credit, the formula is more intentional than you might expect. It uses three branded ingredients: Eriomin® lemon extract, Supresa® saffron and Morosil® red orange. That’s more than some brands on this list can say. The problem is how far the marketing stretches what those ingredients have shown.

Did some digging: The headline ingredient figure is a 17% increase in GLP-1 levels over 12 weeks. That result comes from a small trial of about 30 people who all had elevated blood sugar going in. It isn’t a study of everyday dieters, and it measured a blood marker, not how much weight anyone lost. 17% also sounds bigger than it is. Your GLP-1 level already rises well past that every time you eat a meal.

Then there’s the legal side. Lemme has been hit with class action lawsuits in California and New York. They allege the product was marketed as a natural alternative to GLP-1 drugs without the science to back it up. These are allegations and haven’t been decided in court. Still, it’s not what you want to read about a product you’re about to commit six months to. The brand has since renamed it Reset with the same formula.

The formula is better than the celebrity marketing made me expect. But the ingredient data is not as strong as I’d hope for a $90 bottle, and I’m taking two capsules a day for three to six months before knowing if it’s working. Did I mention I hate pills?

Best Pure Fiber

HUM Flatter Me Fiber GLP-1 Booster

Buy Now
Pros
  • Human study on the fiber at the same amount that’s in the product
  • Drink format
  • Around $30, easy to find at Ulta
  • Natural flavors
Cons
  • Only 2-angle approach to GLP-1 support
  • Study measured one meal, not daily use
  • Enzyme blend is “proprietary”
  • Mixes into 16 oz of water per serving (quite a bit of water for 1 sitting)
  • Not super portable
  • Flavors are OK

HUM is the closest thing on this list to what Wildtype is doing, and I respect it. The main ingredient is a soluble fiber that raised GLP-1 after a meal compared to placebo in a controlled study. The 18-enzyme blend is aimed at bloating, which is a nice extra.

The study behind the fiber is legit, but it looked at a single meal. It tells you what happens that afternoon, not what happens by month three. Plus the ingredients only really target appetite and bloat, so it’s not as all-in-one like other options. They do have an unflavored option which I thought was great but at that point I may be able to just buy fiber and save some money.

On top of the fiber, HUM adds 18 digestive enzymes and a small herbal blend of ginger, fennel and peppermint, all aimed at bloating. That’s where the “Flatter Me” name comes from, and I’d imagine it’s a smart pairing for anyone who gets uncomfortable after bigger meals, more like a digestive aid.

Other Products Tested

ColonBroom GLP-1 Booster

Buy Now
Pros
  • Psyllium is one of the better-studied fibers for fullness and regularity
  • Sugar-free
Cons
  • Psyllium thickens fast, hard to drink
  • The version on Amazon doesn’t match the formula on ColonBroom’s own site
  • Berberine dose is a fraction of research uses
  • Pricing is hidden behind a quiz, with heavy discount framing
  • Parent brand has a D- rating with the Better Business Bureau
  • Two capsules a day

This one confused me more than any other product on the list. ColonBroom built its name on a psyllium fiber powder, and the “GLP-1 Booster” name has been attached to more than one formula. One is a psyllium powder with L-carnitine and Capsimax®. The capsule sold on ColonBroom’s site and at GNC lists barberry bark, resveratrol, quercetin and zinc. A capsule sold on Amazon under the same name lists berberine, chromium, 5-HTP, saffron and bupleurum root. Customers have noticed, and several reviews mention getting a bottle that didn’t match what they thought they’d ordered.

My hesitation was the shopping experience. The bottle lists at $84.99 with savings of up to 76% depending on the plan, and I had trouble figuring out what I’d actually pay each month, and if they can offer 76% then why don’t they just price it lower to begin with?

The bigger issue is the dose. The site’s capsule leads with barberry, the plant berberine comes from. Berberine has real research behind it for blood sugar, but those studies used roughly 900 to 1,500mg a day, split across meals. ColonBroom’s capsule has 200mg of barberry bark powder. That’s the whole ground bark, not a concentrated berberine extract, so the actual berberine in each serving is lower still. Resveratrol and quercetin are popular antioxidants, but neither has meaningful human evidence for appetite or weight at these amounts. And there’s no published study on the finished product itself.

Primal Queen PQ7

Buy Now
Pros
  • Targets appetite from a few angles
Cons
  • Missing fiber
  • Capsules
  • Some users mention gas in the first week
  • Contains dihydroberberine, so check with your doctor if you take blood sugar meds
  • Contains dairy (colostrum), so non-vegan
  • Colostrum is studied at around 20 grams a day, far more than two capsules can hold

PQ7 tries to cover everything at once. The formula pairs colostrum and AstraGin® for the gut with GlucoVantage® dihydroberberine for blood sugar, then adds saffron for cravings, red orange extract, lemon verbena and hibiscus, and chromium. The brand describes this as supporting four fullness pathways.

Seven ingredients sounds impressive until you take a deeper look. Two capsules can only hold so much, and colostrum alone is studied at about 20 grams a day. The math doesn’t work for all seven to be at a meaningful dose, and a couple of the ingredients, like the red orange extract and saffron, have decent research on their own. The problem is you can’t tell whether they’re included at the amounts that research used. The 365-day guarantee is generous, and it’s the strongest reason to try it.

Grüns

Buy Now
Pros
  • Tastes like candy
  • Basic multivitamin content
  • Has 6g fiber
Cons
  • Not designed for appetite or fullness
  • Most are only present in small amounts
  • Costs more than a standard multivitamin

Grüns does a different job from most of the products here. It’s built for people who are already on a GLP-1 medication and struggling to get enough nutrition while eating less. The idea is simple: when your appetite drops, a daily pack of gummies helps fill the gaps a smaller diet leaves.

Ultimately, this is not an appetite or weight product, it’s a nutrition supplementation product, and not even a full multivitamin product. If you want an expensive snack that’s guilt-free to snack on and supplement vitamins, it’s wonderful. If you want help with fullness and cravings, I’d look elsewhere.

Arrae MB-1

Buy Now
Pros
  • Beautiful packaging
  • Available both online and in retail
  • Thousands of reviews
Cons
  • Proprietary botanicals grouped under one total dose
  • Chromium dose unnecessarily high
  • Max version adds 100mg of caffeine
  • Brand says not to combine versions, which makes the lineup confusing
  • Capsule format

Arrae is one of the best-looking brands in the space, and its review numbers are strong. The ingredient list looks solid on paper: African mango (IGOB131®), Cissus (CQR-300®), grains of paradise, green tea, chromium and a probiotic. Several of those are branded ingredients with their own studies. But there’s no way to tell how much of each you’re getting. For a brand that leads with “clinically studied,” I wanted the dose next to each ingredient.

That matters because branded ingredients like IGOB131® were studied at specific daily amounts. If they share one number with three other botanicals, you can’t check whether you’re getting that amount. I also want to flag the “no side effects” promise. It’s a big claim for a formula with green tea extract and, in the Max version, 100mg of caffeine.

Nature Made GLP-1 Companion

Buy Now
Pros
  • About $25 regular price, often less
  • Well-known pharmacy brand
  • Covers the basics: multivitamin, magnesium, B12, calcium and D3
Cons
  • Four pills a day
  • Doesn’t target appetite
  • Designed for people taking the medication
  • Mostly standard vitamins you may already take in a vitamin for 10% the price
  • Many retail reviews are marked as collected through promotions

It’s a daily packet of four pills: a multivitamin, magnesium, B12, and calcium with D3. It doesn’t claim to help with appetite, which is honest, but it does only give 30 servings of what you could probably get in a multivitamin for a fraction of the cost. So the “GLP-1” on the box is doing a lot of the selling. Take away the label and it’s a well-made set of everyday vitamins, and if you already take a multivitamin, you may be doubling up.
