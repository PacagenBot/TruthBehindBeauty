Avoiding minoxidil? You're not the only one

Minoxidil is the best-known hair regrowth drug, but it comes with label warnings many of us want to avoid: it causes initial worsening of shedding, may be harmful while pregnant, is toxic to pets, and you have to keep using it, or hair loss starts again.

Who wants to deal with that headache? Oh, and minoxidil also causes headaches and palpitations in some.

Like, really bad migraines. After all, it was originally a medication for severe high blood pressure that widens your blood vessels (vasodilator), so it can mess with the blood pressure in your head and neck.

We looked at five popular serums that don't contain it, and asked three questions of each:

  1. What's actually in it?
  2. What evidence do they present?
  3. Who is it best for?

Best Overall

RE:YOU Dual-Path Hair Revival Serum

What we liked:
  • The only product tested head-to-head against minoxidil, publicly registered on ClinicalTrials.gov
  • Outperformed minoxidil in a randomized, double-blind trial
  • New molecules, not repurposed drugs like minoxidil
Check Availability
You should know:
  • The most recent data is still an interim readout from the first 90 days of the study

Key ingredients: NOVOGRO™, three newly-discovered molecules found by screening more than 20 million compounds with an AI discovery platform. Two support the dermal papilla cells at the root of the follicle, and one supports the scalp environment around it.

The evidence: 190 participants, compared directly against 2% topical minoxidil. Results were measured with dermoscopy imaging, independent expert grading, comb tests and participant questionnaires, not surveys alone.

At 90 days, the RE:YOU group saw an average of 50% more total hair growth than the minoxidil group. Still, the brand's study is ongoing so results beyond this duration of use haven't been released.

Best for: Anyone who wants the closest thing to minoxidil-level evidence without minoxidil, including people who are postpartum or breastfeeding.

Best Budget

The Ordinary Multi-Peptide Serum for Hair Density

What we liked:
  • You get a lot of well-known hair actives for the price
  • Packed, no nonsense formula where the actives make up 21.15% by weight
Check Availability
You should know:
  • No published study of the finished formula, and all evidence comes from existing research on each separate ingredient

Key ingredients: REDENSYL™, Procapil™, CAPIXYL™, BAICAPIL™, AnaGain™ and 1% caffeine.

The evidence: Ingredient-level only. Each branded complex comes with its supplier's data, but how they perform together in this formula hasn't been studied. So, combining them all does not necessarily improve results. And you wouldn't know this from the product's marketing.

Best for: Budget-conscious shoppers who want to try a peptide-heavy formula and are fine with ingredient-level evidence.

Best for Breakage

Kérastase Genesis Sérum Fortifiant

What we liked:
  • Luxurious feel and elegant formula
  • Specifically targets hair fall from brushing
  • Targets hair breakage due to weak strands beyond the follicle level
Check Availability
You should know:
  • It's designed for breakage related hair-fall, not hair loss from the follicle
  • Their claim "up to 97% less hair-fall" comes from testing the full Genesis hair system against just shampoo
  • 4 'doses' once a day for results. The 30mL bottle only lasts a week

Key ingredients: Ginger root extract, caffeine and arginine.

The evidence: A clinical study of 99 people over 6 weeks, plus instrumental tests. Their main claim comes from testing the whole line of Genesis products (shampoo, serum and hair mask), against only a non-conditioning shampoo. So, the bar is low for comparison and the marketing is trying to convince you to buy 3 products instead of 1.

Best for: People whose shedding is mostly breakage from heat, color, brushing or styling.

Best Botanical

Vegamour GRO Hair Serum

What we liked:
  • Vegan, plant-powered formula
  • Combination of peptides with botanicals
Check Availability
You should know:
  • Reported results are users' perceptions from user perception surveys, not objective measurements

Key ingredients: Red clover, mung bean and Benthamiana extracts.

The evidence: Ingredient-level clinical data plus consumer studies of the finished product. Reading the footnotes, the headline 'up to 52%' density claim refers to ingredients in the formula, tested in an unpublished 40-person study over 150 days, and other claims come from consumer perception surveys.

I would be looking for measurable objective differences in hair density, thickness and regrowth. Those say a lot more than qualitative survey results.

Best for: People who prefer plant-based, botanical products in their routine.

Best for Dermarolling

OneSkin OS-01 HAIR Peptide Scalp Serum

What we liked:
  • Targets cell senescence, a unique scientific approach to hair loss
Check Availability
You should know:
  • Only tested in combination with dermarolling needles, so you need to add that step
  • Small sample size: 36% increase in hair thickness was measured in 6 out of 7 total male participants.

Key ingredients: The OS-01 peptide and supporting ingredients.

The evidence: A 6-month, double-blind, independent study with 30 participants. The brand reports a 36.68% increase in hair thickness among the male participants. The sample size here was very small: 7 male participants total with 6 of them experiencing improvement.

But there's a big caveat: that a dermaroller was used with the serum when tested. So, to get the same results you'd probably need to add the tool as another step.

Best for: People who are drawn to longevity-focused products and are comfortable with dermarolling each time they apply the serum.

What to look for in a minoxidil-free hair serum

Who was tested: the formula or the ingredients?

Many brands cite studies run by ingredient suppliers, not on the product you're buying.

Look for wording like "formulated with ingredients clinically shown to," which signals ingredient-level data. This might not always translate to the formula at hand.

What it was compared against.

A study against a placebo, a regular shampoo or nothing at all tells you less than one against an established treatment.

How results were measured.

Imaging, expert grading and hair counts are more objective than "85% said their hair looked better" surveys.

What it actually targets.

Breakage happens along the strand. Thinning and shedding starts at the follicle. Make sure the product matches your problem.

The extra steps.

Some results depend on add-ons like a dermaroller or a full shampoo-and-conditioner system. Factor those into cost and effort.

Consistency.

Most serums ask for daily use over at least 90 days before judging results. Factors like texture, scent and packaging could impact how likely you are to stay the course and see results.
