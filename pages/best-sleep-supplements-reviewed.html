Melatonin Gummies, Magnesium Pills, or a Multi-Functional Tea? 8 Sleep Supplements, Reviewed.

From a $26 magnesium bottle to a mattress company’s sleep capsule, these are the 8 sleep products people are actually buying in 2026, and which live up to their hype.

Most people with low energy and bad sleep have one of three problems:

  • They can’t shut their brain off at night.
  • They fall asleep fine and then wake up in the middle of the night.
  • They sleep fine, a full 8 hours but still feel like their battery is still on empty

Me personally, I’m in that last category, lights out is no problem but I wake up as if I’ve never even slept.

The sleep aisle mostly answers both problems the same way: melatonin. Melatonin is a timing signal. It tells your body it’s nighttime, which is useful for jet lag or a shifted schedule. It doesn’t calm a racing mind, a broken internal charger, and plenty of people find that higher doses leave them groggy the next morning.

That’s why the category is shifting. Magnesium has become the most popular sleep ingredient online, and a wave of melatonin-free drinks has launched in the past year. Some of them are good. Some are mostly packaging. Here are the 8 worth knowing about in 2026.

Note: these are my personal opinions based on my experience with these products, research, etc. and intended to be a useful tool to make your own informed decisions.

Openly, I only knew about this product because I reviewed their gut support product a few weeks back and saw it on their website. Of all the products tested, most fell short on at least one of three things: ease, experience, or efficacy. Most either knocked me out and left me foggy, or did nothing at all. Deep Restore ultimately had the best balance.

The formula makes sense from an ingredient perspective: L-theanine is an amino acid from tea leaves that helps calm and destress. Deep Restore uses 200mg, four times what’s in the leading melatonin gummy below. The magnesium comes in three forms, bisglycinate, gluconate and citrate, to help the body relax, shown to increase deep sleep, the restorative type. Ashwagandha, lemon balm and the chamomile base round out with herbs people have used at bedtime for centuries.

Fun Fact: In my digging I also found a recent event where they partnered with a magnesium skincare company.

Overall the taste is nice, nothing special but pleasant. It’s sweet-tart apple with a soft herbal finish, and I like it best warm, but it also dissolves in cold water too. Ultimately it’s the only product on this list that both was effective and turned winding down into an actual routine that I kind of liked: drinking a glass an hour before bed while watching TV — something popping a gummy or swallowing pills never did for me. And with no melatonin, I didn’t wake up feeling like I was crawling out of a bunker.

PURE ENCAPSULATIONS Magnesium (Glycinate)

Buy Now
Pros
  • 120mg of magnesium glycinate per capsule, a gentle and well-absorbed form
  • Clean label, single simple ingredient
Cons

  • Pill format

This is one of the best-selling magnesium supplements on Amazon, and it’s easy to see why. It’s simple, well made and reasonably priced, and plenty of practitioners recommend the brand by name. Magnesium glycinate is less likely to cause loose stools than other forms, which matters if other magnesium has sent you running to the bathroom.

If you like pills, and JUST want magnesium this is a solid choice. If you’re looking for a multifunctional solution, this one is more of a building block.

Best Gummy

OLLY Sleep

Blackberry Zen

Buy Now
Pros
  • In nearly every drugstore / big-box store
  • Reasonable melatonin dose
  • Easy to take
  • Tastes good
  • Low cost
Cons
  • NO Magnesium
  • Nominal L-theanine
  • Has sugar
  • Contains gelatin, so it’s not vegetarian
  • Botanical extracts not standardized (on label) and in nominal amounts

OLLY is the sleep gummy most people have tried at least once. Each two-gummy serving has 3mg of melatonin plus L-theanine and a few calming herbs.

The L-theanine and herbs make the label look like a calming blend, but the amounts are small and non standardized. There is no magnesium at all, the 50mg of L-theanine is a quarter of what most sleep studies use, and the herbs are under 20mg each. This is ultimately just a melatonin gummy, and it’s affordable and tastes pretty good. But ultimately melatonin isn’t helping with the restorative part of sleep that I’m missing.

Melatonin is most useful short-term, for jet lag or resetting a schedule. If you’re reaching for it every night, it’s worth asking whether timing is really your problem.

Other Products Tested:

LEMME Sleep

Buy Now
Pros
  • Beautiful packaging
  • Can find at Target and other retailers
  • Easy to take
Cons
  • 5mg of melatonin is a lot
  • 3g of added sugar per serving
  • Nominal Mg & L-theanine

Lemme Sleep has the prettiest jar in the aisle and a long list of calming ingredient names. Looking at the panel, though, the calming ingredients are small. The full serving has 20mg of magnesium, 50mg of L-theanine and a 25mg blend of three botanicals. The melatonin, at 5mg, is again the headline. It’s more expensive than Olly for essentially the same experience.

AG1 AGZ

Buy Now
Pros
  • Melatonin-free
  • NSF Certified for Sport
  • Serious headline ingredients: ashwagandha, L-theanine, saffron and magnesium
Cons
  • $99 a box, or $79 a month on subscription
  • Many reviewers describe a bitter, earthy aftertaste
  • Weird flavors (chocolate)

AGZ is AG1’s nighttime product, and it’s the biggest brand to launch a melatonin-free sleep drink. I’ll be fair: the main ingredients are well chosen, and several are included at amounts you’ll see in research. It also has excellent quality testing.

The formula is strong on paper. The experience is the first area where it struggled for me. It’s one of the most expensive products here and taste is the most common complaint both for me and in reviews. A sleep drink you don’t enjoy is hard to keep up every night.

If you already love AG1 and the taste works for you, AGZ is a serious option with a good ingredient list. If you’re watching your budget, try a sample first.

RECESS Mood

Black Cherry

Buy Now
Pros
  • Feels like a snack, not a supplement
  • 20 calories and no added sugar
  • Widely available in retail
Cons
  • Relaxation, not specifically sleep
  • Only 53mg of magnesium per can

Recess helped create the “calm drink” aisle, and it raised a $30M Series B last year from the investors behind Poppi. Mood is a lightly flavored sparkling water with magnesium and calming ingredients, and it’s a great swap for, say, an evening glass of wine. But it’s a wind-down drink first, not really a sleep product.

WINKS

Goodnight Grape

Buy Now
Pros
  • Melatonin-free
  • Single-serve stick packs
  • One of the most transparent labels in the category, with every dose listed
Cons
  • $65 list price, with constant “up to 57% off” promotions
  • Includes trace lithium orotate, which the brand says has no established sleep benefit at that amount
  • Only one flavor
  • Only 100mg of magnesium

Winks is one of the closest products to Deep Restore on this list, and it deserves credit. Every ingredient has a dose, and the site notes where research doesn’t apply to its amounts. That kind of honesty is rare in this aisle. The saffron and L-theanine are good choices. The trace lithium is the part I couldn’t get past. Even the brand says there’s no established sleep benefit at that amount, so why add it in? This made me question the rest of the product formulation logic. The constant discounting also makes it hard to know what a month really costs. It’s a well-built product, but a busy formula.

EIGHT SLEEP Sleep Elixir

Buy Now
Pros
  • Melatonin-free
  • From a sleep-tech brand with a loyal following
Cons
  • No magnesium at all
  • Proprietary blend with no individual doses or standardizations
  • Form factor (capsules) right before bed
  • Unclear standard dose (1-3 capsules range)
  • $79 list price, though often on sale for $59
  • Its “34% faster” stat comes from users rating their own sleep

Eight Sleep makes the cooling mattress cover a lot of biohackers swear by, and Sleep Elixir is its first supplement. The company says it narrowed 12 candidate ingredients down to four using sleep data from its users. But for a brand built on data, I was surprised the doses aren’t on the label. The 34% number comes from people rating their own sleep, and without the amounts there’s no way to compare it to the research on each ingredient, and doesn’t include key ingredients seen in other products (e.g. there is absolutely no magnesium listed in the product).
