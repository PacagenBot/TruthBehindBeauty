If you’re sleeping like a stranger in your own bed, you’re not imagining it. Maybe you still fall asleep fine but wake up at 3am, wide awake, for no reason. Maybe you’re kicking off the covers, then pulling them back on. Maybe your mind just won’t slow down the way it used to.

Here’s the short version.

During perimenopause, estrogen and progesterone don’t decline in a straight line. They swing up and down, sometimes month to month. Both hormones affect body temperature, mood and the calming signals in your brain that help you stay asleep. When they become unpredictable, so does your sleep. Night waking is one of the most common complaints, and it’s often the hardest to fix.

That’s why the usual answer, a melatonin gummy, often misses the mark here. Melatonin tells your body it’s nighttime. It doesn’t do much for the 3am wake-up, a warm flush, or a busy mind. The products worth looking at for this stage support winding down and staying calm through the night, not just getting knocked out.

In my experience, most women don’t just quit a sleep product because it failed. They also quit because they woke up groggy or forgot to take it. I tried or dug into 5 of the most talked-about options. Here’s where each one landed.

I’m 47, and my sleep changed almost overnight. I could still fall asleep, but I’d wake up sometime around 1–3am, and that was it for the night. Melatonin knocked me out and left me foggy the next morning without fixing the waking up. I heard about Wildtype from a friend going through a different problem, but they had a Magnesium Sleep Tea and figured it was worth a try.

The formula is built around calming the mind rather than sedating you. It has 200mg of L-theanine, an amino acid from tea leaves that helps quiet a busy brain, at the amount used in sleep research. It adds three forms of magnesium, which helps the body relax, plus root-only ashwagandha for everyday stress. Chamomile and lemon balm round it out. There’s no melatonin and no hormones, so it’s an easy add alongside whatever else you’re doing for this stage.

It’s not a menopause-specific product, and it won’t stop a hot flash. What changed for me was the hour before bed. Making a cup became my signal to slow down, and the mental wind-down was what I needed to not only get to sleep, but stay asleep. The nights I drank it were the nights I fell back asleep faster. Waking up clear-headed instead of foggy is what kept me coming back, and I still make the cup most nights.

Overall the taste is nice, nothing special but pleasant. It’s sweet-tart apple with a soft herbal finish, and I like it best warm, but it also dissolves in cold water too. Ultimately it’s the only product on this list that both was effective and turned winding down into an actual routine that I kind of liked: drinking a glass an hour before bed while watching TV — something popping a gummy or swallowing pills never did for me. And with no melatonin, I didn’t wake up feeling like I was crawling out of a bunker.

OLLY is the sleep gummy most people have tried at least once. Each two-gummy serving has 3mg of melatonin plus L-theanine and a few calming herbs.

The problem for perimenopause is the melatonin. It can help you fall asleep, but it’s a timing signal, not a fix for waking at 3am, and plenty of women find it leaves them groggy the next morning. The L-theanine and herbs make the label look like a calming blend, but the amounts are small and non standardized. There is no magnesium at all, the 50mg of L-theanine is a quarter of what most sleep studies use, and the herbs are under 20mg each. This is ultimately just a melatonin gummy, and it’s affordable and tastes pretty good. But ultimately melatonin isn’t helping with the restorative part of sleep that I’m missing. If falling asleep isn’t your problem, this probably isn’t your answer.

The formula is strong on paper. The experience is the first area where it struggled for me. It’s one of the most expensive products here and taste is the most common complaint both for me and in reviews. A sleep drink you don’t enjoy is hard to keep up every night.

If you already love AG1 and the taste works for you, AGZ is a serious option with a good ingredient list. If you’re watching your budget, try a sample first.

Noctera is the one product on this list that says it is built for exactly this problem, and that deserves real credit. It comes from Bonafide, a women’s health brand focused on menopause, and it was tested in a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in women going through perimenopause and menopause. That’s rare in this category.

Did some digging: The research is promising, but keep two things in mind. The study was run and co-authored by the company, and the brand says results take one to two months to build. SAMe is also an ingredient to clear with your doctor if you take an antidepressant or other mood medication. It’s a serious option, but it’s another pill to take every night, with a longer runway than most people expect.
